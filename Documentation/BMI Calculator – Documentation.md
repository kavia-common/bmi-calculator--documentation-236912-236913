# BMI Calculator – Documentation

## 1. Overview

The BMI Calculator helps you estimate your Body Mass Index (BMI) using your height and weight. BMI is a simple number that is commonly used as a general indicator of whether a person’s weight is in a range that may be considered underweight, healthy, overweight, or obese for their height.

This calculator is intended for everyday, informational use. It provides a quick estimate that can help you understand your result at a high level and decide whether you want to discuss your health goals with a qualified healthcare professional.

## 2. What is BMI?

BMI stands for Body Mass Index. It is a number calculated from your height and weight.

BMI does not directly measure body fat, fitness, or overall health. Instead, it is a screening tool that uses weight relative to height. Many health organizations use BMI as a general guideline because it is quick and consistent, but it does not work equally well for everyone.

For example, people with high muscle mass may have a higher BMI even if they have low body fat. Likewise, BMI may not fully reflect health risk for older adults, children, pregnant people, or some individuals with certain medical conditions.

## 3. Inputs Required

To calculate BMI, the calculator needs the following information:

### Height

Your height can be entered in either:
- Centimeters (cm), or
- Meters (m), depending on how the calculator is set up.

Use your most accurate current height if possible. Small differences in height can noticeably change the BMI result.

### Weight

Your weight can be entered in either:
- Kilograms (kg), or
- Pounds (lb), depending on how the calculator is set up.

Use a recent measurement for the most accurate estimate. If your weight changes frequently, consider using an average or your most typical value.

## 4. How the BMI Calculator Works (Formula)

BMI is calculated by dividing weight by height squared.

### Metric formula (kilograms and meters)

BMI = weight (kg) / (height (m) × height (m))

If height is entered in centimeters, it must be converted to meters first:

height (m) = height (cm) / 100

### Imperial formula (pounds and inches)

If the calculator supports imperial units, the commonly used formula is:

BMI = 703 × weight (lb) / (height (in) × height (in))

The calculator performs these steps automatically once you provide your height and weight.

## 5. BMI Categories (Ranges)

After BMI is calculated, it is typically grouped into categories.

The commonly used adult BMI category ranges are:

- Underweight: BMI less than 18.5  
- Healthy weight: BMI 18.5 to 24.9  
- Overweight: BMI 25.0 to 29.9  
- Obesity: BMI 30.0 or higher  

These ranges are general guidelines for adults. They may not apply in the same way to children and teenagers (who use age- and sex-specific growth charts), or to people with certain health conditions.

## 6. How to Use (Step-by-step)

To use the BMI Calculator:

1. Enter your height using the format the calculator requests (for example, centimeters or meters, or feet/inches).
2. Enter your weight using the format the calculator requests (for example, kilograms or pounds).
3. Review your entries to ensure they are correct and in the right units.
4. Select the option to calculate (for example, “Calculate BMI”).
5. Read the result:
   - Your BMI number, and
   - Your BMI category (such as Underweight, Healthy weight, Overweight, or Obesity), if the calculator displays it.
6. If the result is surprising or concerning, consider confirming your height and weight values and calculating again. If needed, talk with a healthcare professional for personalized guidance.

## 7. Examples

### Example 1 (Metric units)

Height: 170 cm  
Weight: 65 kg  

Step 1: Convert height to meters  
170 cm = 1.70 m

Step 2: Apply the formula  
BMI = 65 / (1.70 × 1.70)  
BMI = 65 / 2.89  
BMI ≈ 22.5

Result: BMI ≈ 22.5 (typically in the Healthy weight range)

## 8. Validation & Edge Cases

The calculator is most useful when the inputs are realistic and the correct units are used. Common issues include:

### Incorrect units

A frequent source of unexpected results is mixing units. For example, entering pounds into a field expecting kilograms (or vice versa) will produce a misleading BMI.

If your BMI result seems far too high or too low, double-check that:
- Height is entered in the correct unit (cm vs m, or inches vs feet/inches), and
- Weight is entered in the correct unit (kg vs lb).

### Missing or zero values

If height or weight is missing, zero, or negative, BMI cannot be calculated meaningfully. In those cases, the calculator should prompt you to correct the input.

### Very small or very large values

Extremely small or large values may indicate a typing error. For example:
- Height entered as “17 cm” instead of “170 cm”
- Weight entered as “650 kg” instead of “65.0 kg”

If the calculator accepts such values, the result will not be useful. If you see an unrealistic result, check for a misplaced decimal point or missing digit.

### Children and teenagers

BMI categories for adults are not used the same way for children and teenagers. For younger people, BMI is typically interpreted using growth charts that account for age and sex.

### Athletes and high muscle mass

BMI does not distinguish between muscle and body fat. People with high muscle mass may receive a BMI that falls into a higher category even if their health and body fat level are excellent.

### Pregnancy and certain medical conditions

BMI may not be an appropriate measure during pregnancy or for individuals with certain medical conditions. If you are unsure, consult a healthcare professional.

## 9. Disclaimer

If you have health concerns, symptoms, or questions about your weight or overall health, you should consult a qualified healthcare professional.

## 10. FAQ

### What does my BMI number mean?

Your BMI number is a general estimate of weight relative to height. It is commonly used to group results into categories (such as underweight, healthy weight, overweight, or obesity). It can be a helpful starting point, but it does not capture all aspects of health.

### Why does BMI not match how healthy I feel?

BMI is only one measurement. It does not measure fitness, muscle mass, body fat distribution, or overall well-being. Someone can have a higher BMI and still be physically fit, and someone can have a “healthy” BMI but still have health risks.

### Can BMI tell me my body fat percentage?

No. BMI is not a body fat measurement. It is a screening calculation based on height and weight.

### What should I do if my BMI is high or low?

If your BMI is outside the typical “healthy weight” range, consider:
- Checking that you entered your height and weight correctly (and in the right units).
- Using the result as a general prompt to learn more about healthy habits.
- Speaking with a healthcare professional for advice tailored to your situation.

### How often should I calculate BMI?

You can calculate BMI whenever you want to track changes over time, such as after changes in diet, activity, or weight. For many people, checking occasionally (for example, monthly or quarterly) is sufficient, but the right frequency depends on your personal goals.
